Components

In the above table, a plethora of components are given. All of these components are explained below in their respective sections

Manual Resource Mining

This is a Vessel component/feature that allows said Vessel to manually mine resources in the vessels mining range. This consists of a tool that fires a beam on a target Ore to melt pure metal out of it. Then this mining system can pull the water towards it to collect the freshly mined metal. The Vessel needs to be manually brought in front of the target ore and the Beam only has a range of 10m.

This implies, the vessel needs to be within a range of 10m to the target Ore to mine it. Narrative Background: This is an extremely cheap and easy to build system often found in mining vessels. In the game, only the Vessel name DIVE 880 will be equipped with such a tool.

Universal Automatic Resource Mining

This is a mining utility in which a Vessel can collect resources without manually locating itself in front of a resource. With this feature, the player simply needs to press a button to collect resources without having to look anywhere. The player just needs to be in the vicinity and it will work.

Navigation Screen

This screen consists of features like objective markers, friendly vessels, search zones, target depths, target distances etc.

This screen has nothing to do with SONAR. It’s really just a minimap of sorts that does not tell a lot about the map but more about the Vessels and the Entities in it.

Mining Camera

This is basically a camera that can look into the 3D world. These cameras have a very limited visibility. These cameras cannot see further than 50. Other types of cameras may be able to look a little further and not a lot.

Proximity Sensors

This is an array of sensors that detect the proximity of any underwater obstacles like rocks, ocean bed or even other vessels from the vessel towards 6 different directions. These directions being Front, Back, Left, Right, Down and Up. The sensors light up and make a beep sound if the ship is closer than a 100m to anything. These sensors also show the distance to said obstacles.

Remote Camera

This is a camera that is launched from the vessel and can be possessed by the player. The camera can be piloted and placed at a specific location and the feed can be viewed from the Vessel.

SONAR Decoys

These are utility items that can be dropped at a location and they create a fake SONAR signal to confuse enemies or hostile fauna of your real location

Directional Aquatic Microphone (DAM)

This is a in game mic that can be rotated into any direction to very clearly listen to specific sounds from precise directions.

Sound Wave Analysis

This feature is paired with the DAM. When the DAM detects and locks on to a sound source, this feature can run a sound wave analysis and show the wave form of the sound. This can help detect what exactly is making the unknown sound in the ocean.

Armaments Rooms

These are essentially rooms that contain the weapon firing surfaces and tubes of the Vessels. They also hold the weapons of the Vessel.

If a Vessel only has a Bow Armament Room, then the Vessel can only fire weapons like torpedoes facing the front side. The torpedoes can of course be controlled later but their launch location and initial direction is basically what is affected by room locations.

Light Torpedoes

These can be launched from one or the other Armaments Rooms. They are quick and do not do enough damage to kill but three of these torpedoes can kill a Vessel. Since these are quick, it’s a little difficult to steer these in time.

Damage Amount - 2500

These Torpedoes cause physical damage directly to the Hull.

EMP Torpedoes

These can be launched from one or the other Armaments Rooms. They are moderately quick (Not as quick as Light Torpedoes but faster than Heavy Torpedoes) and do not do any damage to the hull. These Torpedoes damage target electronics only.

Damage Amount - 1000 These do enough damage to temporarily incapacitate enemy electronics in one hit.

Heavy Torpedoes

These can be launched from one or the other Armaments Rooms. They are slow and clunky and not easily maneuverable. These can kill a light vessel in one hit and almost kill a heavy Vessel in one hit.

Damage Amount - 4000

These do damage directly to the Hull

Mines

These are stationary hazards that can be deployed from Armaments Rooms. One hit from an enemy mine translates to immediate death.

Damage Amount - 10000

Damages the hull.

Vessel Features/Property Description

The following section contains descriptions, details and explanations of various features given in the table above

Hull Strength

Hull strength is basically the total HP of the submarine. If the hull strength runs out, the Vessel will depressurize and will get destroyed. Game Over. Collisions with the ocean floor or some sort of a rock causes physical damage. Torpedoes cause a lot of physical damage. Mines can do enough damage to destroy a vessel in one hit. Hull strength cannot be regained once lost.

Electronics Integrity

This essentially is another HP pool separate from Hull Strength. Physical collisions do not affect Electronics. As the Vessel runs out of Electronics HP, ship systems dependent upon Electronics HP will start shutting down. Electronics HP can only be affected by fishes that can cause electrostatic damage, underwater volumes that are electrically supercharged, or by EMP torpedoes. Electronics Integrity can be repaired in certain vessels.

Energy Reserves

This reserve acts as fuel for the Vessel’s various systems. It is needed for pretty much everything like Propulsion, Detection, Navigation etc.

If this reserve gets depleted, dependent Vessel systems will momentarily stop till it regenerates energy. Some vessels cannot regenerate energy.

Propulsion Systems

Some vessels have two propulsion systems, a Primary and a Secondary propulsion system. Propulsion systems are Energy Reserves and Electronics Integrity dependent.

Propulsion Systems HP will be visible only in some ships. In ships, that cannot see the propulsion systems HP, when the Energy Reserves or the Electronics Integrity depletes, propulsions systems will simply shut down until said dependency can be repaired or replenished.
